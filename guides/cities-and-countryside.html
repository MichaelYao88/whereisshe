Guessing cities and the countryside

The clues change when the frame is a street, and they change again when it is a field. whereisshe.gg uses both.

In a city, read before you recognise

A downtown round is full of text. Street blades, shop names, bus routes, tram wires, metro entrances, and the language on a pharmacy are all better than the skyline. Skylines are copied in memory. A sign is in the clip. Start with the same five checks: script, driving side, road paint, buildings, sun. In a city the first two usually finish the job, and the buildings tell you the neighbourhood only after the country is clear.

Density is a clue of its own. Continuous shop fronts, painted bike lanes, pedestrian signals, and overhead wires suggest a large city. A single commercial street with fields behind it is a town. Do not pin the capital just because the street looks busy. Capitals are a small target. The right large city, or the right side of the right country, matches how scoring actually works: 30 kilometres is perfect, and a few hundred kilometres still keeps most of the points.

A bus livery, a metro typeface, or a bike-share dock can name a city when you truly know it. If you only half remember it, confirm with the language on the stop. Global car brands do not. A Toyota in frame is not Japan.

In the countryside, widen the pin on purpose

A field, a coast, a desert highway, or a mountain pullout may have no readable text at all. Guessing a city then means guessing a dot you cannot see. That is how rounds go very wrong. When the words are missing, decide the climate and the driving side, then pin a region, not a downtown.

Plants and soil do more work here. Rainforest, dry scrub, cactus, vineyards, rice paddies, birch, and red dirt each cross many borders, and together with driving side they still cut the map. Fences, gate styles, and the quality of the asphalt tell you something about how rural the road is. A sealed highway with a yellow line and right-side driving is a different shortlist from a dirt track with left-side driving and eucalyptus. Mountains tell you elevation and sometimes a range, once you know the continent. They rarely tell you a town by shape alone.

A beach with palms is a climate, not an island. If the frame has no writing, pin the coast you can defend inside the country you can defend. Naming a resort from memory is the trap.

Same trip, different rounds

A whereisshe.gg day is one person's trip, told in five rounds. A city round and a countryside round can sit next to each other. After the first reveal, use the country you learned, and do not assume every later pin is the same city. Rounds are sometimes a flight away.

If you are unsure whether you are looking at a suburb or a village, choose the smaller claim. Pin the metro area when the clues are urban and unnamed. Pin the rural region when the clues are fields and a road. Both of those are closer, on the curve, than a confident pin on the wrong famous place. Then read the distance after you guess and see which kind of clue was actually in the frame. Reading a clip is the closer look at signs, light, and sound when you want to slow down.
