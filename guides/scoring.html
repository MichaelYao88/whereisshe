How scoring and distance work

What the points mean after you press Guess on whereisshe.gg.

One pin, one distance, one score

Each round is one photo or short clip and one pin. You can move the pin as often as you like. Nothing is scored until you press Guess. The game then measures how far that pin is from the place the photo was taken and turns the distance into points. A round is worth up to 5,000 points. A day has five rounds, so a perfect day is 25,000. There is no timer. Looking longer, zooming a still photo, or replaying a clip does not cost points.

How the distance is measured

The distance is the short path along the surface of the Earth, a great-circle distance. It is not the driving route, and it is not a chord through the inside of the planet. whereisshe.gg uses the haversine formula with an Earth radius of 6,371.0088 kilometres. Longitude is wrapped so a guess near the date line is scored along the short way around, not the long way. After you guess, the map draws a dashed line from your pin to the answer along that same short path, including when the line crosses water.

Full points inside 30 kilometres

A pin within 30 kilometres of the real spot scores the full 5,000. The reveal marks that as perfect. The radius is intentional. A clip is often filmed in a park, on a beach, or beside a road, and a pin on the right town is already a strong guess. You do not need the exact camera position. The curve meets 5,000 at that 30 kilometre edge, so there is no cliff the moment you step outside it. Scores are whole numbers, so a fraction of a kilometre past 30 can still round to 5,000. A little farther and the score starts to fall, a few points at a time.

The curve past 30 kilometres

Past the radius, the score is 5,000 times e raised to the power of minus (distance minus 30) divided by 1,492.7, then rounded to the nearest whole number. The 1,492.7 figure is the scale of that curve in the game, in kilometres. Close misses stay high. Far misses fall off and then flatten, because there are fewer points left to lose.

A few round distances, taken from that formula and not from anyone's results: about 100 kilometres is roughly 4,770 points, about 500 kilometres is roughly 3,650, about 1,000 kilometres is roughly 2,600, about 2,500 kilometres is roughly 950, and about 5,000 kilometres is under 200. Missing a nearby city can still be an excellent round. Missing by a continent cannot.

Colours, metres, and the archive

A shared result uses wider bands than the perfect radius. Under 100 kilometres is a green square, under 500 is yellow, under 2,000 is orange, and anything farther is red. A yellow square can still be thousands of points. The square is a quick read for a friend. The number on the results screen is the score from the curve.

Very short distances are shown in metres. Under 10 kilometres the game keeps one decimal place. Longer distances are rounded to the nearest kilometre. Archive days use this same measurement and the same curve. Finishing an archive day stores that score in your browser and does not change your daily streak. The streak moves only when you finish today's daily. The daily and archive guide covers that split, and using the map covers the pin itself.
